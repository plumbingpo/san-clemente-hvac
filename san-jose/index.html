How San Jose Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that lasts and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a new filter every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your equipment plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A weak capacitor costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, ignored through a full season, can ruin the compressor and force a full system replacement. Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in San Jose

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of problems. Long cooling seasons push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and humidity and airborne grit slowly foul outdoor condenser coils, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Clogged filters quietly starve systems of airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

We also see the full range of component failures in local homes: weak capacitors that leave the condenser humming without starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much less expensive when caught early.
